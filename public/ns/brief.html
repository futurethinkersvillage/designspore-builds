The short version

Lead with a small, concrete ask in his own language: a three-week Network School popup on our land in summer 2027. It's a build sprint that ends in an art festival, housed in zomes we fabricate on site, with local artists funded from day one. If it works, it graduates to a permanent node.

That fits his popup-to-permanent sequence and the gaps he has right now: North America, summer, local support and culture. It also avoids his red flags: partying, degrowth, back-to-the-land and sovereignty grabs.

What we learned

  1. Network School lost Malaysia in July 2026. Johor revoked its business licence effective July 22. The trigger was an activist claim that Israelis were on campus; immigration checked 266 members and found valid papers. The formal grounds were licensing and use of premises (classrooms in an office-licensed unit). It became unrecoverable because of three things: promos that described the site as "an island near Singapore" without naming Malaysia, no local constituency to defend it, and Balaji publicly freezing a planned RM500M expansion.

  2. This corrects our starting assumption. The cause wasn't simply that locals felt left out. The trigger was political, but having no local stakeholders is why nobody spoke up for it. The art and local-grant angle still holds; it's about building that support before you need it.

  3. Network School is now in Kazakhstan. A 5-year agreement with the government; the Astana campus opened Sept 2. Balaji is also exploring a "Network City" in Burabay, a lakes-and-forest resort region, built from "modular infrastructure integrated into the natural landscape." That's the closest thing to our land in his portfolio.

  4. His sequence is popup, then permanent. From "Popups are the new startups" (Oct 2025): a popup is "a physical prototype" and a permanent site is "a production deployment." His rule for land: cloud first, land last — but not land never.

  5. His funding runs through Network School. Step 5 of his roadmap: Start your own society… We intend to fund the best. Ask small and evidence-based (a popup, a pilot, a listing), not "fund our land."

  6. His own book backs art and a Burning Man equivalent. we create art and literature that reflects our values. And thanks to Starlink, a community can build their own Burning Man equivalent in the middle of nowhere for permanent habitation. Network School and Kazakhstan also just ran a $2M AI film festival with about 8,000 entries.

  7. He dislikes partying, degrowth and back-to-the-land. He wrote that no one was coming to Network School for the 'nightlife.' His book sets "back-to-the-land" anarcho-primitivism against his own transhumanism. Never pitch "regenerative", "reconnect with nature", "food sovereignty" or festival vibes.

  8. Learn, burn, earn. Fitness is a pillar: proof-of-workout and Blueprint-style food. Our sauna, cold plunge, trails, mountain biking and cross-country skiing fit "burn" directly.

  9. Open-source prefab nodes are on his roadmap. Version 4 of Network School is a portable template that we can open source and recreate anywhere. With the CNC, the zome plans, the Dome & Zome Generator and the WikiHouse configurator, we can be the workshop that prototypes his nodes.

  10. Education and measurement. He praises land-grant colleges, which were built to teach frontiersmen how to run farms, dig mines, and erect buildings, and Network School now offers childcare and tutoring. He also measures everything: applications vs. places (4,000 for 128), members, cohorts, countries. Bring numbers, not vibes.

The ecosystem around him

  • Edge City runs monthlong popup villages (12,500+ participants so far) and is listed on Network School's dashboard. Its 2026 calendar was Esmeralda (May–June), then India and Bhutan (Oct–Nov). There is no Canadian village and nothing in July–September. The biggest complaint at Esmeralda is that a $3K ticket doesn't include housing.
  • Esmeralda is our best comparable: 266 acres in Sonoma County with a planned hotel, event centre, preschool and amphitheatre. They ran popups first, then two years of town halls. The council vote was set for the week of Oct 7, 2026.
  • "Ark" is ambiguous. Ârc is Network School's first permanent spin-off, a charter-city project with popups in Lisbon and Austin. It is not an eco-village. The ARK in Costa Rica is a regenerative village, but we found no link to Balaji. Worth checking which one you heard about at the conference.
  • Where his money has gone: Pronomos Capital (Próspera), the Praxis seed round, the Afropolitan pre-seed, the Balaji Fund (a rolling fund launched in 2025) and the Network School Fellowship ($100K for up to 100 founders).
  • Otherworld is BC's official Burning Man regional, run by Kindle Arts. It's capped at 3,200 people with roughly three times that demand, and dropped general admission in 2026. It currently runs at Laketown Ranch on Vancouver Island.

The Canada question

He will think about this, so we answer it before he asks. In 2022 he pointed to Canada's trucker-convoy account freezes as the reason Bitcoin matters. After Malaysia he says he only wants to work with "a willing counterparty."

  • Private titled land and an operating, licensed resort. We are not asking for a special zone.
  • A small town that wants this. The District of Clearwater signed an agreement with Simpcw First Nation in April 2025. Mike getting more involved with the Clearwater Chamber of Commerce would strengthen this further.
  • In Vitalik's terms (which Balaji promoted), our site is a monastery: the retreat node that complements his city embassies. Kamloops airport is 1.5 hours away, with Starlink across the property.

Your festival questions, answered

What if Network School were affiliated with a Burning Man–style festival?

It would get the culture layer its own definition asks for: "a sense of national consciousness" and "a capacity for collective action." Burning Man shows a temporary city of 80,000 can govern itself, build its own infrastructure and grow a shared culture every year. It has no economy and no permanence, and Network School has both. The affiliation joins the ritual to the economy.

Why would members choose a festival over another conference?

  • Co-creation bonds people. You don't bond in an audience; you bond building a camp together at 2 a.m.
  • It's a rehearsal for a network state. Infrastructure, logistics, rules and a census, in miniature, every year.
  • Output, not talk. Art, structures, prototypes, films and ventures get shipped.
  • It reaches the people Network School is missing: artists, women, families and North Americans.
  • Burn. Trails, the lake, sauna, cold plunge and mountain air.
  • Lore. The stories and images people retell for years, which a society runs on.

What a Network School festival on our land would look like

Working name: NS North. Housing is included (zomes, glamping and RV sites), which fixes Esmeralda's biggest complaint.

Weeks 1–2 · Build

Members, local artists and our makerspace build the art and the village: CNC, LED, zome raising. Funded by art grants.

Weeks 2–3 · Learn & burn

Talks, a hackathon, proctored maker credentials. Morning trail runs, sauna and cold plunge.

Week 3 · Ship

Demo day at the amphitheatre and an AI-film night on the drive-in screen.

Finale · Light

A three- to four-day art and music festival, open to the region with free local days. BC fire bans make LED art the signature.

What the deck must include and avoid

Include

  • His own words, quoted exactly
  • The popup-to-permanent ladder
  • Hard numbers: 400 acres, 5 years operating, ~3,000 guests a year, 45 RV sites, a 120-person gazebo, a 10,000-person list, 10M+ downloads
  • Access: Kamloops airport 1.5 h, Starlink, hydro power, our own wells
  • Local roots: 5 years operating, local hiring, the Clearwater–Simpcw agreement as the model
  • A metrics slide, and a small first ask with a larger ladder behind it

Avoid

  • Naming Malaysia
  • "Regenerative", food "sovereignty", "back to nature", anti-screen language
  • Festival-as-party imagery: crowds, DJs
  • Any sovereignty claim
  • A big equity ask up front
  • Claiming a Chamber of Commerce role
