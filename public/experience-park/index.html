02The Shift

The old script is breaking

A generation ago the plan was simple — a big house, a good school, and you were set. It doesn’t hold anymore: schools are slipping, cities feel less safe, kids are lost in screens, and no one really knows how to prepare a child for a post-AI world. You can’t just buy the bubble.

What families are looking for now
Real, hands-on skillsthe kind AI can’t hand you
A healthy relationship with techtools, not a leash
Real time in naturespace, sky, water, quiet
A capable communitypeople who build and grow
Time out of the citysomewhere that feels alive

So we’re building the place that gives families exactly that.

06Maker Culture

Most of it, we make

At the heart of the park is a working maker space — CNC, laser, and a full shop where the cabins, furniture, sculptures, and signage all get built on-site.

Built on the land Cabins, furniture, art, and the signage you see — made here, from local materials.
Hands-on learning Where kids and adults pick up real skills — the seed of our forest-school program.
Skills for a post-AI world Making, fixing, and building — the things worth knowing when everything else is automated.

Most of what we need, we can make — and teach.

07Food & Energy Sovereignty

Grown, made, and powered here

Not off-grid survivalism — just genuinely less dependent than a city. Some of it is here today; the rest is where we’re headed.

Power

Hydro-dam grid — clean, local, close to source.

Water

Our own wells, pumps, and a private lake.

Grow planned

A garden today — a food forest and greenhouses to come.

Raise

Meat from neighbouring ranches.

Connect

Starlink internet with property-wide Wi-Fi.

Build

A maker space to make and mend what we need.

A place that keeps working when things wobble — and a taste of a more self-reliant way to live, whether or not you ever need it.

08The Land

400 acres, already alive

We already own and operate a 400-acre resort in Wells Gray, BC — water, power, roads, staff, and guests are in place.

A private lake, creeks, forest trails, and a 120-person gazebo — and we’re minutes from Wells Gray Provincial Park: waterfalls, hiking, and wilderness at the doorstep.

Room to grow into all of it, one season at a time.
